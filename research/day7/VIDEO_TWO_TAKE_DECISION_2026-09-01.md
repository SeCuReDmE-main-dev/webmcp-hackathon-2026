# Day 7 trace — two-take video decision

Date: 2026-09-01
Timezone: America/Toronto
Status: accepted plan; production pending
Decision owner: Jean-Sebastien Beaulieu

## Question

How can the final Devpost video prove genuine browser-agent interaction in the
first seconds while preserving human authority and keeping production simple?

## Decision

The final video will use two source takes. Jean-Sebastien submits the production
mission before recording. Codex prepares the clean screen, live QCG page, Chrome
side panel and Screencastify, remains active, and activates full-screen recording
through approved window control only when it can immediately invoke the first
native QCG tool. Prompt submission and model latency remain outside the retained
take. The second source is a voice-over recorded by Jean-Sebastien after the
screen proof has passed technical review.

NotebookLM leaves the required production path. It may support rehearsal or
private transcript work, but the final technical proof will be the real Codex,
Chrome and WebMCP interaction.

The accepted target is 2:50–2:55. The screen take follows two connected evidence
lifecycles—quantum preflight followed by a bounded Codex-to-Gemini review—rather
than visiting controls without a narrative purpose. The first native action
appears within 10 seconds and the take is rejected if it exceeds 12 seconds.
The opening identifies the footage as live screen proof with narration recorded
afterward, preventing a staged voice-over from being mistaken for the trigger.

Companion open, same-tab attachment and same-session synchronization are now
recording gates. The retained take includes a genuine Codex observation, a new
sanitized packet, a real native-Gemini response, preview, untrusted import and
human acknowledgement. Failed extension interactions, permission prompts,
fabricated agent attribution and recovery attempts stay outside the retained
cut.

## Human-authority boundary

Codex may inspect, evaluate, wait, simulate after consent and export evidence.
It may never click Accept, Defer, Override, Remember or Forget. During the
recording, Codex stops at the decision gate. Jean-Sebastien performs the visible
click in the QCG page. Codex continues only after the conditional simulation
tool is registered. In the A2A segment, Codex and Gemini may observe and
challenge; Jean-Sebastien alone acknowledges the review, and that collaboration
action cannot change quantum consent.

## Why this is stronger

- It satisfies Devpost's request to show the working product and agent tool use
  within the first 10–15 seconds.
- It removes the predictable delay between submitting a Codex prompt and the
  first browser action.
- It removes dependence on a generated explainer as the source of proof.
- It lets the narration describe an already observed result.
- It makes the separation among agent recommendation, human authority and local
  execution visible rather than merely stated.
- It reduces recording risk to one clean technical take and one clean audio take.

## Article impact

The Summer article will treat this decision as the moment the demonstration
became structurally identical to the product: the agent acts, the human decides,
and the receipt preserves what happened. The Day 7 narrative will also retain
the separate work completed for Professor Aguilar as a human/editorial episode
about respecting authorship and responsibility. Aguilar's scientific content
will remain outside QCG's technical evidence.
