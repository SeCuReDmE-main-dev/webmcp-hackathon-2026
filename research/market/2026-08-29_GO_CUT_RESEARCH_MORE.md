# WebMCP-QCG — GO narrow / CUT general / RESEARCH MORE

Date: 2026-08-29

## Decision headline

**GO** with a browser-native human-in-the-loop quantum preflight workbench.
**CUT** the general multi-provider execution-platform narrative.
**RESEARCH MORE** before treating QCG as a commercial product.

## Why the narrow product deserves a GO

1. The local prototype already proves native WebMCP discovery, deterministic decisions, progressive capability, bounded Q# simulation and portable evidence.
2. Primary sources confirm expensive or delayed quantum calls create a real guardrail problem: Braket enforces spending limits, Open Quantum exposes a quote/confirm step, IBM encourages transpilation and usage estimation, and Quantinuum documents long hardware queues.
3. The browser provides a distinctive human-authority surface. A researcher can see the artifact, the decision, the reason codes, the evidence and the consent state while the agent receives compact tools.
4. An executionless gate remains technically feasible within the hackathon because it carries no provider credentials, performs one bounded local simulation and treats readiness as a report state.

## Why the general product receives a CUT

The market already contains:

- qBraid Agent Mode with an internal MCP, live device/queue/pricing context, multi-framework coding and hardware approval;
- official Qiskit MCP servers for circuit work, transpilation, documentation and Runtime execution;
- Open Quantum MCP with `prepare_job`, quote, spend preview and separate submission;
- provider-native compilers, estimators, dashboards, spending caps and provenance systems.

A claim such as “the first multi-provider quantum agent router” would be inaccurate. Building that platform would also place credentials, live pricing, provider contracts, arbitrary programs and paid calls into the seven-day critical path.

## Narrowest defensible differentiation

> WebMCP-QCG is a provider-neutral, browser-native, executionless evidence firewall. It decides whether an external quantum call is justified, keeps technical readiness separate from human authorization, and exports a portable receipt before credentials, upload or provider submission.

The decision contract is:

```text
artifact digest
+ scientific intent
+ requested bounds
+ exact prior-evidence key
+ timestamped target-profile snapshot
        ↓
deterministic compatibility, freshness, reuse and budget policies
        ↓
one decision
+ reason codes
+ next action
+ authority state
+ profile and receipt hashes
+ local/external call counters
```

## Research-more gate

Recruit five researchers or advanced developers who have used at least two quantum SDK or provider surfaces. Give each one real, non-sensitive artifact and ask them to compare their normal preflight with QCG.

Pass criteria:

- at least 90% agreement with an expert reference across twenty fixed decision cases;
- zero false `ready_for_external_execution` results;
- zero external calls for reuse, reject, recompile and simulate-first decisions;
- at least three of five users identify a real omission or repeat-run risk through the receipt;
- at least two of five want to install or integrate the gate before provider calls.

If users prefer qBraid or Open Quantum and see no independent value in the receipt, QCG should pivot into a receipt specification or validation module instead of a standalone platform.

## Public claim set

Safe now:

- “QCG proved one browser-native WebMCP preflight path with a bounded local Q# simulation.”
- “The recorded trace made one local simulation and zero external provider calls.”
- “QCG separates readiness from authorization and exports a decision receipt.”

Evidence required later:

- professional time or money saved;
- multi-provider compatibility;
- live price or queue accuracy;
- scientific correctness beyond the bounded fixture;
- market demand or willingness to pay.
