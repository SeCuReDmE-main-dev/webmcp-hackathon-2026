## Proof contract

The video demonstrates one complete browser-native execution loop and one
bounded collaboration loop:

```text
real Q# artifact → inspect → evaluate → visible human authorization
→ bounded local simulation → portable evidence receipt

Codex observation → sanitized handoff → native Gemini reply
→ untrusted import → visible human acknowledgement
```

It makes four boundaries explicit:

- WebMCP carries the structured browser interaction.
- Deterministic application code owns inspection, policy and simulation.
- The researcher owns authorization and interpretation.
- The MVP performs zero provider, paid-compute or QPU submissions.
- Agent-to-agent discussion can challenge evidence, but cannot change quantum
  consent, memory or execution authority.

## Production contract

The final video is assembled from exactly two source takes:

1. **Take 1 — screen proof.** The production prompt is submitted before the
   recording. Codex performs a private preflight, stays active, activates
   Screencastify through approved window control at the exact evidence boundary,
   and immediately controls Chrome through the side panel. No prompt latency or
   narration is retained in this take.
2. **Take 2 — voice-over.** Jean-Sebastien records the narration after the
   screen take has passed QA. This makes the spoken story follow the evidence
   instead of forcing the evidence to follow a prerecorded script.

NotebookLM is optional for private rehearsal or transcript checking. It is not
part of the required final-video path.

The voice-over is openly asynchronous. Add the discreet opening caption
`Live screen proof • narration recorded afterward`. Jean-Sebastien may narrate
the genuine interaction in present tense, including phrases such as “I ask
Codex to inspect this experiment,” but the edit must never make a prerecorded
or simulated voice command appear to have triggered the screen actions.

## Editing rules

- Preserve the chronological truth of the tool calls and human click.
- Remove every loading or idle-polling frame from the retained cut; never
  reorder causality.
- Add short captions for `inspect`, `evaluate`, `human decision`, `simulate`
  and `receipt`, followed by `WebMCP lifecycle`, `accessible clarity` and
  `activity trail`.
- Keep the opening disclosure that narration was recorded afterward; never cut
  the voice-over to imitate a live voice command.
- Remove Gemini response latency, but retain the real packet, real reply and
  chronological handoff boundary.
- Keep zoom and crop stable enough for tool names and counters to remain legible.
- Use no copyrighted music; silence under the voice-over is acceptable.
- Export at 1080p and verify the measured duration.

## Claims allowed on screen

- Four progressive WebMCP tools.
- Five deterministic recommendation outcomes.
- Real Q# artifact inspection and byte-bound SHA-256.
- Bounded local Q# WebAssembly simulation.
- One-use, expiring and revocable human consent.
- Bell invariant pass for the demonstrated fixture.
- Zero external provider calls and zero QPU submissions in the retained proof.
- Browser-local Access preferences that alter presentation without altering
  decision state, paired with semantic names, roles and states shared by direct
  and agent-assisted use.
- Fifty-one automated tests from a clean clone and the official live WebMCP
  smoke, if shown with current receipts.

## Claims to avoid

- Guaranteed cost savings.
- Universal compatibility across quantum providers.
- Hardware execution or QPU validation.
- Autonomous scientific authority.
- Production security certification.
- Accessibility certification or any claim that the Access panel replaces
  assistive technology, semantic implementation or manual testing.
- Any suggestion that OpenAI, Google, IBM, Microsoft, NVIDIA or a hackathon supporter endorses QCG.
